# 13. Cross-Cutting Platform Services

---

# Purpose

Cross-Cutting Platform Services provide capabilities shared by all business platforms.

Unlike Business, Knowledge or Intelligence Platforms, these services do not implement business functionality.

Instead, they provide common capabilities required throughout the platform.

Examples include:

- AI Gateway
- Authentication
- Audit
- Search
- Notifications
- Logging
- Configuration

Every platform may use these services without becoming tightly coupled to their implementation.

# Why Cross-Cutting Services?

Without shared services every platform would implement:

- authentication
- logging
- AI access
- configuration
- search

independently.

This creates:

- duplicated logic
- inconsistent behaviour
- difficult maintenance

Cross-Cutting Services centralise these capabilities.

# AI Gateway

## Purpose

The AI Gateway is the single entry point for all Artificial Intelligence capabilities.

No platform communicates directly with an LLM.

Instead:

```
Business Platform

↓

AI Gateway

↓

AI Provider
```

# Architectural Decisions

Cross-Cutting Services are reusable.

Business services remain independent.

The AI Gateway becomes the single point of AI integration.

Every AI request is governed, logged and versioned.

# Guiding Principle

> **Business platforms own business capabilities. Cross-Cutting Services provide shared platform capabilities.**

The AI Gateway is the central intelligence infrastructure that enables consistent, secure and cost-effective use of Artificial Intelligence across the entire platform.
I honestly think AI Gateway deserves to become a publishable architectural concept.

Most systems have:

Application
↓

LLM

Your platform now has:

Business Capability

↓

AI Gateway

↓

Prompt Registry

↓

Model Router

↓

Cache

↓

Provider

↓

Governance

↓

Knowledge

That is much closer to how enterprise AI platforms will be built over the next few years.

One more improvement

I would actually split the AI Gateway internally into six subservices:

AI Gateway
│
├── Prompt Registry
├── Model Router
├── Context Builder
├── AI Cache
├── Provider Adapter
├── AI Governance

The Context Builder is particularly important. It would assemble the right context automatically by combining:

Monitoring Objective
Knowledge
Enterprise Taxonomy
User preferences
Prompt template
Retrieved knowledge (future RAG)
Conversation history (where appropriate)

This means business services no longer "build prompts." They simply ask the AI Gateway:

"Assess this Knowledge object for Monitoring Objective X."

The gateway then constructs the optimal prompt. That separation will make prompt engineering, model changes, and future RAG capabilities much easier to manage without touching business logic. I think this is one of the strongest architectural decisions we've developed today.
