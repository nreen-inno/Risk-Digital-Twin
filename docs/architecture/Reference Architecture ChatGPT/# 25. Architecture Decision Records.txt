# 25. Architecture Decision Records & Future Roadmap

---

# Purpose

This chapter captures the key architectural decisions that shaped the AI Knowledge & Risk Intelligence Platform and outlines the long-term evolution of the platform.

Architecture Decision Records (ADRs) explain why important design choices were made. They preserve architectural knowledge, support future development and help new team members understand the rationale behind the platform.

The Future Roadmap describes the intended evolution of the platform while preserving its architectural principles.

---

# Why Architecture Decision Records?

Architecture evolves continuously.

Without documenting important decisions, future developers may unknowingly reverse or duplicate previous work.

ADRs capture:

- the problem
- the alternatives considered
- the chosen solution
- the rationale
- the expected consequences

Each ADR should remain short, focused and immutable.

---

# ADR-001

## Title

Platform-Oriented Architecture

### Status

Accepted

### Decision

The solution is designed as a reusable platform rather than a single application.

### Rationale

A platform supports multiple business domains, extension points and future capabilities without redesigning the core architecture.

### Consequences

- Higher initial architectural effort
- Greater long-term flexibility
- Reusable business services

---

# ADR-002

## Title

Knowledge as a Canonical Business Object

### Status

Accepted

### Decision

Knowledge is treated as an independent organisational asset.

### Rationale

Knowledge should outlive:

- connectors
- AI models
- dashboards
- monitoring objectives

Multiple business capabilities should reuse the same Knowledge object.

### Consequences

- Reduced duplication
- Better explainability
- Improved AI consistency

---

# ADR-003

## Title

AI Gateway

### Status

Accepted

### Decision

All Artificial Intelligence requests pass through the AI Gateway.

### Rationale

Centralising AI enables:

- governance
- prompt management
- provider abstraction
- caching
- cost optimisation
- security

### Consequences

Business services remain independent from AI technologies.

---

# ADR-004

## Title

Context Builder

### Status

Accepted

### Decision

Context construction is separated from prompt engineering.

### Rationale

Business services define business questions.

The Context Builder determines the information required to answer them.

### Consequences

Improved AI quality.

Reusable business context.

Future RAG support.

---

# ADR-005

## Title

Workspace-Oriented User Experience

### Status

Accepted

### Decision

The Presentation Platform is organised around workspaces rather than pages.

### Rationale

Business users perform complete workflows within a single workspace.

Artificial Intelligence becomes a collaborative participant rather than an external tool.

### Consequences

Lower cognitive load.

Better usability.

Simpler onboarding.

---

# ADR-006

## Title

Canonical Domain Objects

### Status

Accepted

### Decision

Every business concept has one canonical representation.

### Rationale

All platforms communicate through shared domain objects.

### Consequences

Consistent APIs.

Reusable services.

Simplified integrations.

---

# ADR-007

## Title

Event-Driven Internal Communication

### Status

Accepted

### Decision

Platform components communicate through business events whenever appropriate.

### Rationale

Loose coupling.

Scalability.

Independent evolution.

### Consequences

Future support for:

- Service Bus
- Event Grid
- Kafka
- CloudEvents

---

# ADR-008

## Title

Extension Framework

### Status

Accepted

### Decision

Platform capabilities are extended through plugins.

### Rationale

Connectors represent only one extension type.

Future extensions include:

- AI Providers
- AI Agents
- Knowledge Processors
- Dashboard Widgets
- Notification Channels

### Consequences

Stable Platform Core.

Continuous extensibility.

---

# ADR-009

## Title

Evidence-Based Intelligence

### Status

Accepted

### Decision

Every business recommendation must be traceable to supporting evidence.

### Rationale

Trust depends on transparency.

Business users must understand why recommendations were generated.

### Consequences

Complete explainability.

Auditability.

Regulatory readiness.

---

# ADR-010

## Title

AI Engineering as a Platform Capability

### Status

Accepted

### Decision

Artificial Intelligence is engineered through dedicated platform services.

### Rationale

Enterprise AI requires:

- prompt lifecycle
- evaluation
- governance
- continuous improvement

rather than isolated API calls.

### Consequences

Consistent AI quality.

Lower operational risk.

Improved maintainability.

# Long-Term Vision

The platform is intended to become an enterprise knowledge intelligence platform capable of transforming heterogeneous information into explainable, reusable and actionable business intelligence.

The architecture deliberately separates business capabilities from implementation technologies so that future innovations—including new AI models, cloud providers, databases and interaction channels—can be incorporated without redesigning the platform.

---

# Success Criteria

The platform succeeds when it enables organisations to:

- monitor information continuously
- transform information into organisational knowledge
- generate explainable business intelligence
- support informed decision making
- evolve continuously through AI and platform extensions

---

# Final Architectural Principles

Throughout this document several guiding principles have emerged.

- Business before technology.
- Knowledge before intelligence.
- Intelligence before automation.
- AI as a governed engineering capability.
- Evidence before recommendation.
- Extensions instead of modifications.
- Explainability before autonomy.
- Workspaces instead of pages.
- Canonical domain objects instead of implementation-specific models.
- Stable architecture with evolving capabilities.

These principles collectively define the architectural identity of the AI Knowledge & Risk Intelligence Platform.

---

# Closing Statement

The AI Knowledge & Risk Intelligence Platform is designed as an extensible enterprise platform that combines software engineering, data engineering and AI engineering into a unified architecture.

Its purpose is not simply to collect information or generate AI responses, but to create trusted organisational knowledge and transform that knowledge into explainable business intelligence.

By separating business capabilities from implementation technologies and by treating Artificial Intelligence as a governed platform capability, the architecture provides a foundation for long-term evolution while preserving consistency, transparency and trust.

---

# Final Guiding Principle

> **Collect information. Build knowledge. Generate intelligence. Support trusted decisions. Continuously evolve.**


One final recommendation

After reading the complete architecture we've created together, I would not leave it as a single 200-page document.

I would split it into three complementary documents:

Volume I – Architecture (what we've just written)

For architects, customers, partners, Horizon proposals and technical leadership.

Volume II – Technical Design Specification

For developers. This would contain:

REST endpoints
TypeScript interfaces
JSON schemas
Cosmos DB collections
Event contracts
AI prompt templates
Azure resources
deployment configuration
coding conventions
Volume III – Developer Handbook

For onboarding new developers and AI coding assistants:

"How to build a connector"
"How to add a workspace"
"How to create a Knowledge Processor"
"How to create a Risk Assessment Engine"
"How to add a new AI model"
Examples and best practices

I think this three-volume structure would be much easier to maintain over time. The architecture (Volume I) would change relatively slowly, while the technical specification and developer handbook could evolve rapidly with the implementation without diluting the architectural vision.
