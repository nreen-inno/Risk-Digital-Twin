# Appendix A – Glossary

---

# Purpose

This glossary defines the common terminology used throughout the AI Knowledge & Risk Intelligence Platform architecture.

The glossary establishes a shared vocabulary for business users, architects, developers, researchers and AI coding assistants.

Where possible, definitions describe the business meaning of a term rather than its technical implementation.

---

# A

## Adapter

A software component responsible for communicating with a specific external technology or protocol (for example RSS, REST API, MQTT or Azure IoT Hub).

Adapters are used by Connectors to retrieve information.

---

## AI Gateway

The central platform service responsible for all Artificial Intelligence interactions.

The AI Gateway manages:

- prompt execution
- context construction
- model selection
- provider abstraction
- governance
- caching
- auditing
- cost optimisation

Business services never communicate directly with AI models.

---

## AI Provider

A service that executes AI models.

Examples include:

- Azure OpenAI
- Azure AI Foundry
- OpenAI
- Anthropic
- Google Gemini
- Local language models

---

## AI Request

A canonical object representing one request submitted to the AI Gateway.

---

## AI Response

The canonical result returned by the AI Gateway after AI processing.

---

# B

## Business Platform

The platform responsible for managing business configuration.

Examples include:

- Monitoring Objectives
- Monitoring Profiles
- Information Sources

---

## Business Workspace

A user workspace focused on completing a business task.

Examples include:

- Source Workspace
- Monitoring Workspace
- Risk Workspace

---

# C

## Canonical Object

The single authoritative representation of a business concept throughout the platform.

Every business object has one canonical definition regardless of storage technology or API implementation.

---

## Connector

A software component responsible for acquiring information from an external or internal information source.

Connectors transform source-specific data into canonical Raw Records.

Connectors do not perform Knowledge Processing or Risk Assessment.

---

## Connector Definition

The executable runtime configuration of a Connector.

---

## Connector Proposal

An AI-generated recommendation describing how a new connector should be implemented.

---

## Connector Specification

The approved business and technical design of a connector.

It is produced from a Connector Proposal after user review.

---

## Context Builder

A core component of the AI Gateway responsible for assembling the complete business context required for AI reasoning.

Typical context includes:

- Knowledge
- Monitoring Objective
- Enterprise Taxonomy
- User Preferences
- Platform Configuration
- Historical Information

The Context Builder is independent of the selected AI model.

---

## Correlation ID

A unique identifier shared by all processing steps belonging to the same business operation.

It enables complete traceability across:

- APIs
- Connectors
- Knowledge
- Risk Signals
- Recommendations
- Logs

---

# D

## Dashboard

A presentation workspace providing summaries, trends and key performance indicators.

Dashboards consume business intelligence rather than raw information.

---

## Domain Object

A business object representing a concept within the platform.

Examples include:

- Information Source
- Knowledge
- Risk Signal
- Recommendation

---

# E

## Entity

A recognised object extracted from information during Knowledge Processing.

Examples include:

- organisations
- people
- countries
- shipyards
- suppliers
- regulations

---

## Event

A business occurrence identified during Knowledge Processing.

Examples include:

- strike
- accident
- regulation published
- supplier bankruptcy
- political decision

---

## Event-Driven Architecture

An architectural style where platform components communicate through published business events instead of direct service calls.

---

## Evidence

The information supporting a Knowledge object, Risk Signal or Recommendation.

Evidence provides explainability and traceability.

---

## Extension Framework

The architectural framework enabling new platform capabilities through reusable plugins rather than modifications to the Platform Core.

---

# G

## Governance

Policies, controls and processes that ensure platform capabilities remain secure, explainable, auditable and trustworthy.

Governance includes AI Governance, Knowledge Governance and Operational Governance.

---

# I

## Information Source

Any internal or external system from which information is collected.

Examples include:

- news websites
- ERP systems
- IoT platforms
- email
- weather services
- government portals

---

## Intelligence Platform

The platform responsible for transforming Knowledge into business intelligence.

Outputs include:

- Risk Signals
- Recommendations

---

# K

## Knowledge

A canonical business object representing reusable organisational understanding extracted from one or more Raw Records.

Knowledge is independent of individual connectors, AI models and dashboards.

Knowledge is considered a long-term organisational asset.

---

## Knowledge Platform

The platform responsible for creating, storing and managing Knowledge.

---

## Knowledge Repository

The logical storage responsible for persisting Knowledge objects.

Future implementations may include:

- document databases
- knowledge graphs
- semantic indexes

---

# M

## Model Router

A component of the AI Gateway responsible for selecting the most appropriate AI model for each request.

---

## Monitoring Objective

A business objective describing what the organisation wants to monitor.

Examples include:

- supplier stability
- geopolitical developments
- regulatory changes
- environmental risks

---

## Monitoring Profile

A reusable configuration describing how monitoring should be performed.

Typical configuration includes:

- keywords
- entities
- locations
- languages
- exclusions

---

# P

## Platform Core

The stable foundation of the platform.

Business capabilities are extended through the Extension Framework rather than modifications to the Platform Core.

---

## Presentation Platform

The platform responsible for user interaction.

It presents business capabilities through workspaces rather than traditional application pages.

---

## Prompt

A structured instruction submitted to an AI model.

Within this platform prompts are managed through the Prompt Registry and executed exclusively through the AI Gateway.

---

## Prompt Registry

The central repository of version-controlled prompt templates used throughout the platform.

---

# R

## Raw Record

The canonical representation of information collected from an Information Source.

Raw Records preserve source information without business interpretation.

They form the foundation for Knowledge Processing.

---

## Recommendation

A suggested business action generated from one or more Risk Signals.

Recommendations remain advisory unless an organisation explicitly enables automated execution.

---

## Relationship

A connection identified between two or more Entities during Knowledge Processing.

---

## Repository

A logical component responsible for persisting and retrieving canonical business objects.

Repositories encapsulate storage implementation details.

---

## Risk Assessment

The process of evaluating Knowledge against Monitoring Objectives to determine potential business impact.

---

## Risk Signal

A canonical business object representing an identified business risk.

Risk Signals are generated from Knowledge and Monitoring Objectives.

---

# S

## Semantic Index

A future platform capability supporting semantic retrieval of Knowledge using embeddings or similar technologies.

The Semantic Index is an implementation component rather than a business object.

---

## Service

A backend component responsible for implementing one business capability.

Business logic resides within Services.

---

## Source Workspace

The primary workspace for onboarding and managing Information Sources throughout their lifecycle.

---

# T

## Traceability

The ability to follow every business conclusion back to its originating evidence.

Typical traceability chain:

Recommendation

↓

Risk Signal

↓

Knowledge

↓

Raw Record

↓

Connector Execution

↓

Information Source

---

# U

## User Workspace

A dedicated environment supporting one business activity.

Workspaces combine user interaction, AI assistance and backend services into a single coherent experience.

---

# V

## Versioning

The controlled evolution of business objects, prompts, connectors and platform components while preserving historical traceability.

---

# W

## Workspace

The primary interaction model of the Presentation Platform.

A workspace groups all functionality required to complete a business process.

Examples include:

- Dashboard Workspace
- Source Workspace
- Monitoring Workspace
- Knowledge Workspace
- Risk Workspace
- Operational Workspace

---

# Guiding Principle

> **Every architectural term has one authoritative definition. Every business object has one canonical meaning. Every participant—human or AI—uses the same vocabulary.**
