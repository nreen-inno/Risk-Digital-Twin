# 01. Architectural Principles & Design Decisions

---

# Purpose

This chapter defines the fundamental architectural principles that govern the design and implementation of the Risk Digital Twin (RDT) platform.

These principles are intended to support consistent architectural decisions throughout the lifetime of the project. Every new feature, connector, AI capability or user interface component should comply with these principles.

---

# Architecture Philosophy

The Risk Digital Twin is designed as an **AI-native knowledge platform** rather than a traditional monitoring application.

Traditional monitoring platforms primarily collect information and display alerts.

The Risk Digital Twin continuously transforms heterogeneous information into reusable organisational knowledge and business intelligence.

Instead of asking:

> *What happened?*

the platform ultimately answers:

> *Why does it matter, what is the business impact, and what should we do next?*

---

# Core Architectural Principles

## Principle 1 — Separation of Responsibilities

Every architectural component has a single, clearly defined responsibility.

No component should perform tasks belonging to another architectural layer.

### Connector

Responsible for:

- Collecting data
- Mapping data into the canonical RawRecord format

Not responsible for:

- Business interpretation
- Risk assessment
- Recommendations

---

### Knowledge Extraction

Responsible for:

- Identifying entities
- Detecting events
- Extracting relationships
- Building reusable knowledge

Not responsible for:

- Risk assessment

---

### AI Enrichment

Responsible for:

- Semantic understanding
- Classification
- Summarisation
- Confidence estimation

Not responsible for:

- Source collection
- Dashboard presentation

---

### Risk Signal Engine

Responsible for:

- Business interpretation
- Risk categorisation
- Severity estimation
- Impact assessment
- Recommendation generation

---

### Dashboard

Responsible only for presenting information.

Business logic should never be embedded inside frontend components.

---

# Principle 2 — Knowledge before Intelligence

The platform separates knowledge extraction from business reasoning.

Traditional architecture:

```text
Document
    ↓
Risk
```

Risk Digital Twin architecture:

```text
Document
    ↓
Knowledge
    ↓
Business Intelligence
```

Knowledge becomes reusable across:

- Multiple Monitoring Objectives
- Multiple dashboards
- Future AI agents
- Future Knowledge Graph

Knowledge should never be regenerated if it already exists.

---

# Principle 3 — Canonical Data Model

Every information source is transformed into the same internal representation.

Regardless of whether the source is:

- RSS
- REST
- Database
- Email
- ERP
- IoT
- Weather
- SAP

the output becomes a **RawRecord**.

This simplifies downstream processing and allows reusable AI pipelines.

---

# Principle 4 — AI-first Design

Artificial Intelligence is considered a core architectural capability.

AI participates in:

- Connector recommendation
- Connector refinement
- Knowledge extraction
- Semantic enrichment
- Risk signal generation
- Recommendation generation

AI is not an isolated service but a platform-wide capability.

---

# Principle 5 — Monitoring Objectives Drive Processing

Monitoring Objectives define business intent.

They determine:

- Which information is relevant
- Which sources are assigned
- Which Monitoring Profiles are used
- Which Risk Signals should be generated

Connectors remain generic and reusable.

---

# Principle 6 — Monitoring Profiles Drive Relevance

Monitoring Profiles contain reusable filtering logic.

Examples include:

- Keywords
- Organisations
- Locations
- Languages
- Exclusion rules
- Sensitivity level

Monitoring Profiles should remain independent from connectors.

One connector may serve multiple Monitoring Profiles.

---

# Principle 7 — Connectors Collect, They Do Not Think

Connector implementations should remain intentionally simple.

A connector should:

- Connect
- Fetch
- Parse
- Map
- Store

Nothing else.

Business intelligence belongs later in the processing pipeline.

---

# Principle 8 — Evidence-Based Decisions

Every Risk Signal must be fully traceable.

Traceability chain:

```text
Risk Signal
    ↓
AI Enrichment
    ↓
Knowledge
    ↓
RawRecord
    ↓
Information Source
```

This enables:

- Explainability
- Auditing
- Regulatory compliance
- AI transparency

---

# Principle 9 — Configuration is Separate from Runtime

Configuration objects include:

- Information Source
- Connector Proposal
- Connector Specification
- Connector Definition
- Monitoring Objective
- Monitoring Profile

Runtime objects include:

- Connector Execution
- RawRecord
- Knowledge
- AI Enrichment
- Risk Signal

Configuration changes should not affect historical runtime data.

---

# Principle 10 — Extensibility by Design

New capabilities should be added by extension rather than modification.

Examples include:

- New connector types
- New AI models
- New dashboards
- New recommendation engines
- New risk taxonomies

Existing services should require minimal modification.

---

# Principle 11 — Cloud Native

The platform is designed for cloud-native deployment.

Services should be:

- Stateless where possible
- Independently deployable
- Independently scalable
- Independently testable

Azure-native services are preferred but architecture should remain portable.

---

# Principle 12 — AI Governance

AI-generated outputs should always include:

- Confidence
- Supporting evidence
- Version of AI model
- Processing timestamp

AI outputs should be reproducible and auditable.

---

# Principle 13 — Progressive Automation

The platform supports multiple levels of automation.

Level 1

Human decisions.

Level 2

AI recommendations.

Level 3

AI-assisted automation.

Level 4

Autonomous monitoring.

The architecture should support gradual progression without redesign.

---

# Principle 14 — Future Knowledge Layer

Knowledge is treated as a first-class architectural asset.

The Knowledge Layer enables:

- Semantic search
- Knowledge Graph
- Cross-source correlation
- Historical reasoning
- Multi-objective analysis
- AI agents

Although the initial implementation may store knowledge in simplified structures, the architecture anticipates future evolution toward a reusable organisational knowledge repository.

---

# Architectural Decision Summary

| Decision | Rationale |
|----------|-----------|
| Canonical RawRecord | Simplifies downstream processing |
| Separate Knowledge Layer | Enables reuse across multiple objectives |
| Generic Connectors | Reduces maintenance and increases scalability |
| Monitoring Profiles | Decouples filtering from collection |
| AI-first Architecture | Maximises automation while keeping business logic explainable |
| Traceable Risk Signals | Supports auditing and user trust |
| Modular Services | Allows independent evolution of platform components |
| Configuration separated from Runtime | Protects historical data and simplifies versioning |

---

# Guiding Principle

Every architectural decision should support the following vision:

> **Collect information once. Extract knowledge once. Reuse knowledge many times. Generate business intelligence where it creates value.**
