# 15. Governance, Security & Trust

---

# Purpose

The Governance, Security & Trust Architecture defines the policies, controls and mechanisms that ensure the AI Knowledge & Risk Intelligence Platform operates in a secure, transparent, explainable and trustworthy manner.

Unlike traditional security architectures that focus primarily on authentication and network protection, this architecture also governs Artificial Intelligence, Knowledge, Recommendations and Business Decisions.

Trust is considered a first-class architectural capability.

---

# Objectives

The platform shall provide:

- Security
- Privacy
- Explainability
- Accountability
- Governance
- Compliance
- Traceability
- AI Transparency
- Operational Trust

---

# Governance Architecture

```
Business Policies
        │
        ▼
Platform Governance
        │
        ▼
AI Governance
        │
        ▼
Operational Governance
```

Governance spans every platform.

---

# Identity & Authentication

Every platform user must be authenticated.

Supported identities may include:

- Microsoft Entra ID
- Local accounts
- Service accounts
- External identity providers

Authentication should remain independent from business logic.

---

# Authorization

Permissions are granted according to business roles.

Typical roles include:

- Risk Manager
- Project Manager
- Researcher
- Connector Developer
- Platform Administrator
- AI Administrator

Permissions should follow the principle of least privilege.

---

# AI Governance

Artificial Intelligence is governed centrally through the AI Gateway.

Responsibilities include:

- approved models
- prompt version control
- provider selection
- model routing
- token monitoring
- cost monitoring
- response logging
- policy enforcement

Business services never communicate directly with AI providers.

---

# Prompt Governance

Prompts are treated as controlled platform assets.

Every prompt has:

- unique identifier
- version
- owner
- creation date
- approval status
- intended purpose

Prompt changes should be auditable.

---

# AI Explainability

Every AI-generated output should include:

- reasoning
- confidence
- model
- prompt version
- timestamp
- supporting evidence

Users should always be able to understand why an AI conclusion was produced.

---

# Knowledge Governance

Knowledge is considered an organisational asset.

Knowledge governance includes:

- ownership
- lifecycle
- confidence
- evidence
- quality
- provenance

Knowledge should never exist without evidence.

---

# Recommendation Governance

Recommendations are advisory.

The platform supports:

Human Review

↓

Human Approval

↓

Future Autonomous Approval

The architecture allows organisations to determine the desired level of automation.

---

# Audit Trail

Every important operation generates an immutable audit record.

Examples include:

- Monitoring Objective created
- Connector approved
- Connector activated
- Prompt updated
- Knowledge reprocessed
- Recommendation accepted
- Recommendation dismissed

Audit records support compliance and incident investigation.

---

# Traceability

Every Recommendation must remain fully traceable.

```
Recommendation
        │
        ▼
Risk Signal
        │
        ▼
Knowledge
        │
        ▼
Raw Record
        │
        ▼
Connector Execution
        │
        ▼
Connector Definition
        │
        ▼
Information Source
```

The platform should never produce conclusions that cannot be traced to evidence.

---

# Data Security

The platform protects:

- configuration
- runtime data
- knowledge
- business intelligence
- credentials
- AI configuration

Encryption should be applied both at rest and in transit.

---

# Secrets Management

Sensitive information includes:

- API keys
- connector credentials
- certificates
- AI provider credentials

Secrets are stored only within a dedicated secrets management service.

Application code never stores secrets.

---

# Privacy

The architecture supports privacy by design.

Capabilities include:

- data minimisation
- configurable retention
- anonymisation
- access control
- auditability

Privacy requirements should be configurable according to organisational policies.

---

# Compliance

The architecture supports future compliance with organisational and regulatory requirements.

Examples include:

- GDPR
- ISO 27001
- NIS2
- organisational policies

Compliance mechanisms remain configurable.

---

# Trust Framework

Platform trust is based on five pillars.

```
Evidence

↓

Knowledge

↓

Transparency

↓

Explainability

↓

Human Oversight
```

These pillars support user confidence in AI-assisted decision making.

---

# Human Oversight

Artificial Intelligence supports decision making.

Final responsibility remains with authorised users unless an organisation explicitly enables autonomous operation.

The architecture supports progressive automation rather than mandatory automation.

---

# Governance Metrics

Examples include:

- prompt versions
- AI model versions
- recommendation acceptance rate
- human overrides
- knowledge confidence
- audit completeness
- policy violations

These metrics support continuous governance.

---

# Future Evolution

The Governance Architecture anticipates:

- AI policy engines
- autonomous agents
- organisational governance policies
- explainable multi-agent reasoning
- regulatory reporting

These capabilities extend governance without changing business architecture.

---

# Architectural Decisions

Security is integrated into every platform.

Governance extends beyond infrastructure.

Knowledge is governed as an organisational asset.

Artificial Intelligence operates under policy control.

Business users remain accountable for business decisions.

---

# Guiding Principle

> **Trust is created through transparency, evidence, governance and human oversight—not through Artificial Intelligence alone.**

The Governance, Security & Trust Architecture ensures that every business decision produced by the platform remains secure, explainable, auditable and worthy of organisational trust.
One last architectural improvement

I think we've reached the point where the platform has five fundamental assets. These are what the system ultimately exists to manage:

Configuration — what the organization wants to monitor.
Information — what the platform collects.
Knowledge — what the platform understands.
Intelligence — what the platform concludes.
Trust — why users are willing to rely on those conclusions.

That fifth asset—Trust—is missing from most AI architectures. By making it explicit, your platform distinguishes itself from systems that simply generate AI outputs. Instead, it manages the entire chain from evidence to trusted decisions, which aligns perfectly with the architecture you've developed.
